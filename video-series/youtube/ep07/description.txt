256t is defined as much by what it leaves out as by what it does. Each omission is a trade-off, and seeing them side by side with Git, IPFS, BitTorrent and friends is the clearest way to understand the design.

We walk through what the spec deliberately puts beyond its scope: finding and publishing (including the draft t256: URI scheme, which names content without naming a server), metadata, permissions, chunked Merkle-tree hashing, and algorithm agility. Each one buys something, like one name per sequence of bytes and an implementation that fits on a slide.

Then we place 256t among its neighbors: data: URIs, Subresource Integrity, Git's length-plus-hash blobs, RFC 6920 ni: URIs, BitTorrent magnet links, and IPFS. We finish by putting the whole series back together, and an invitation: pick a file you care about, and compute its name.
