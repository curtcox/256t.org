Three content identifiers that look like noise, and by the end you can read them by hand. Every number in the 256t format, 8, 64, 86, 94 and even the "256" in the name, falls out of a little base64 arithmetic.

We start with bits and bytes, and the URL-safe base64 alphabet of 64 characters at 6 bits each. Three bytes turn into exactly four characters, so the single byte "A" becomes "QQ". The first 8 characters of every CID are the content's length: 48 bits, exactly six bytes, read like an odometer with base-64 wheels. That's why so many CIDs start with a run of A's, and why the limit is 2^48 bytes, or 256 terabytes.

Next comes the fingerprint: 512 bits of SHA-512 need 86 characters, and 8 + 86 = 94. Plot the cost of writing small content out directly against the flat cost of a hash, and they meet at exactly 64 bytes. At or below that, the CID simply contains the content. We decode all three mystery names, and see that the whole recipe fits on one slide.
