So where does Shaw five-twelve stand? It's been around for over two decades, it's one of the most studied functions in cryptography, and no collision for it has ever been found.

Researchers do probe it, the way they probed M D five and Shaw one. They attack weakened versions with fewer rounds. The best practical published collisions reach twenty-seven of its eighty steps. In an even more favorable setting, where the attacker also gets to choose the internal starting state, which a real attacker can't, they reach thirty-nine steps. That's a meaningful distance from the full eighty. And its birthday bound starts at two to the two hundred fifty-six, so even a shortcut that knocked off a huge chunk of that would leave an astronomical number behind.

Could that change someday? In principle. But if history is a guide, we'd see the cracks coming years ahead.
